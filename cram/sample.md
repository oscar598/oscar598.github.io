# Biology 101 · Unit 3 Notes: Cells and Energy

## Cell Theory
Cell theory states that all living things are made of cells, the cell is the basic unit of life, and all cells come from pre-existing cells. Robert Hooke first described cells in 1665 after looking at cork under a microscope. Matthias Schleiden concluded in 1838 that all plants are made of cells. Theodor Schwann extended the idea to animals in 1839. Rudolf Virchow proposed in 1855 that all cells come from other cells.

Prokaryote: a single-celled organism that lacks a nucleus and membrane-bound organelles.
Eukaryote: an organism whose cells contain a nucleus and membrane-bound organelles.
Bacteria and archaea are the two domains of prokaryotes. Most prokaryotic cells are between 1 and 10 micrometers across, while most eukaryotic cells are between 10 and 100 micrometers across.

## Organelles
The nucleus is the organelle that stores the cell's DNA and controls gene expression. The nucleolus is a dense region inside the nucleus where ribosomes are assembled. Ribosomes are the molecular machines that build proteins by linking amino acids. The rough endoplasmic reticulum is studded with ribosomes and folds proteins destined for membranes or secretion. The smooth endoplasmic reticulum makes lipids and detoxifies drugs. The Golgi apparatus modifies, sorts, and packages proteins into vesicles. Lysosomes are membrane sacs filled with digestive enzymes that break down worn-out parts. The mitochondrion is the organelle that produces most of the cell's ATP through cellular respiration. The chloroplast is the organelle that captures light energy for photosynthesis in plants and algae. The cell wall is a rigid layer outside the membrane that gives plant cells their shape and is made mostly of cellulose.

## The Cell Membrane
The plasma membrane is a phospholipid bilayer that controls what enters and leaves the cell. Phospholipids have hydrophilic heads that face the water and hydrophobic tails that face each other. The fluid mosaic model describes the membrane as a flexible layer with proteins drifting within it. Cholesterol keeps the membrane fluid at low temperatures and stable at high temperatures. Selective permeability is the property of letting some substances cross the membrane while blocking others.

## Transport Across Membranes
Diffusion is the movement of particles from an area of high concentration to an area of low concentration. Osmosis is the diffusion of water across a selectively permeable membrane. Facilitated diffusion is passive transport that uses channel or carrier proteins to move molecules down their gradient. Active transport is the movement of molecules against their concentration gradient using energy from ATP. The sodium-potassium pump moves 3 sodium ions out of the cell and 2 potassium ions into the cell for each ATP used. Endocytosis is the process by which a cell takes in large particles by wrapping them in membrane. Exocytosis is the process by which vesicles fuse with the membrane to release their contents outside the cell.

A hypertonic solution has a higher solute concentration than the cell, so the cell loses water and shrinks. A hypotonic solution has a lower solute concentration than the cell, so water rushes in and animal cells can burst. An isotonic solution has the same solute concentration as the cell, so there is no net movement of water.

## Enzymes
Enzymes are proteins that speed up chemical reactions by lowering the activation energy. The substrate is the molecule an enzyme acts on. The active site is the pocket on an enzyme where the substrate binds. Most human enzymes work best near 37 degrees Celsius and a pH of about 7. Denaturation is the loss of an enzyme's shape, and therefore its function, caused by extreme heat or pH. A competitive inhibitor blocks the active site so the substrate cannot bind. A noncompetitive inhibitor binds elsewhere on the enzyme and changes the shape of the active site.

## Photosynthesis
Photosynthesis is the process that converts light energy, water, and carbon dioxide into glucose and oxygen. Chlorophyll is the green pigment that absorbs red and blue light. The light-dependent reactions take place in the thylakoid membranes and produce ATP, NADPH, and oxygen. The Calvin cycle takes place in the stroma and uses ATP and NADPH to fix carbon dioxide into sugar. Rubisco is the enzyme that fixes carbon dioxide in the Calvin cycle and is the most abundant protein on Earth. The oxygen released during photosynthesis comes from splitting water molecules.

## Cellular Respiration
Cellular respiration is the process that breaks down glucose to release energy stored as ATP. Glycolysis is the first stage of respiration; it happens in the cytoplasm and splits one glucose into two pyruvate molecules. Glycolysis produces a net gain of 2 ATP per glucose. The Krebs cycle takes place in the mitochondrial matrix and releases carbon dioxide. The electron transport chain is located in the inner mitochondrial membrane and produces most of the ATP. Oxygen is the final electron acceptor in the electron transport chain. Aerobic respiration can yield about 36 ATP per glucose molecule. Fermentation is the anaerobic process that regenerates NAD+ so glycolysis can continue without oxygen. Lactic acid fermentation happens in human muscle cells during intense exercise, while alcoholic fermentation happens in yeast.

## The Cell Cycle
The cell cycle is the ordered series of events a cell goes through to grow and divide. Interphase is the longest phase of the cell cycle, during which the cell grows and copies its DNA. Mitosis is the division of the nucleus into two identical nuclei. The four stages of mitosis are prophase, metaphase, anaphase, and telophase. During metaphase, chromosomes line up along the middle of the cell. During anaphase, sister chromatids are pulled to opposite poles. Cytokinesis is the division of the cytoplasm that follows mitosis. Cancer is uncontrolled cell division caused by mutations in genes that regulate the cell cycle. A human body cell has 46 chromosomes.
